## ABSTRACT 

Transformer-based tabular foundation models (TFMs) achieve state-of-the-art per-formance on small to medium-sized tabular prediction tasks while processing di-verse inputs through a shared stack of layers. Recent layerwise analyses identify strong predictive performance in early TFM representations and substantial over-lap in computation across layers. However, early predictive accessibility alone does not establish whether later layers are dispensable for every input, and ag-gregate accuracy can obscure which inputs benefit from additional computation. We study all 24 in-context-learning layers of TabPFN-3 across 272 classification datasets, combining representation geometry, prediction trajectories, truncation, and structural interventions, while also examining related representation dynam-ics in four additional TFMs. We uncover a pronounced expansion-compression 

trajectory, with stronger late dimensional contraction associated with larger in-creases in class-prototype separation. A targeted analysis of early-stabilizing sam-ples shows that substantial representational changes persist after predicted classes stabilize, while locally ambiguous queries obtain larger accuracy gains from shal-low to late layers than well-separated queries. Repeating a single pretrained block or perturbing layer order reduces predictive performance on average, constrain-ing simple post-hoc substitutions in the frozen model. These findings distinguish predictive accessibility, class stability, and the benefits of continuing computation, supporting an input-dependent assessment of depthwise redundancy in TFMs. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Tabular foundation models (TFMs) (Hollmann et al., 2023; 2025; Qu et al., 2025; Garg et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b; Ma et al., 2025) use in-context learning to predict on new datasets from labeled support examples, without task-specific parameter updates. A single pretrained model processes tasks with different feature spaces and class structures through the same stack of in-context-learning layers. Understanding how much different inputs benefit from this depth is important for assessing computational redundancy and identifying opportunities to reduce inference cost while preserving prediction quality (Elbayad et al., 2020; Fan et al., 2020). Recent layerwise analyses identify strong predictive performance in early TFM representations and substantial overlap in computation across layers (Balef et al., 2026). TabSwift (Liu & Ye, 2026) further demonstrates per-query early exiting and observes that deeper exits concentrate in ambiguous regions of representation space. These findings motivate examining what subsequent computation still contributes. Early predictive accessibility does not establish that later layers are dispensable for every input, and aggregate accuracy can obscure which inputs benefit from them. Our layerwise evaluation of TabPFN-3 makes this distinction concrete. When queries are grouped by local class separation within each dataset, locally ambiguous groups show larger absolute ac-curacy gains from L9 to L23 on average than well-separated groups (Figure 1 (a)). Meanwhile, a targeted analysis of early-stabilizing samples shows that their representations continue to change 1arXiv:2610.19814v1 [cs.LG] 14 Oct 2026 Preprint substantially after their predicted classes stabilize, including pronounced late dimensional contrac-tion (Figure 1 (b)). These observations highlight the need to distinguish predictive accessibility, class stability, and the accuracy gains from additional computation. They motivate our central ques-tion: When does later computation remain predictively useful, and how does its value vary across inputs? 0 20 40 60 80 100

We study all 24 ICL layers of TabPFN-3 using 272 classification datasets from OpenML-CC18 (Bis-chl et al., 2019) and TALENT (Liu et al., 2025). We complement its native attention-based retrieval decoder (Grinsztajn et al., 2026) with layer-matched classifiers and decoder-independent probes, combining representation geometry, prediction trajectories, truncation, and structural interventions. We also examine related representation dynamics in four additional TFMs (Hollmann et al., 2025; Grinsztajn et al., 2025; Qu et al., 2025; 2026). We find that TabPFN-3 representations follow a pronounced expansion-compression trajectory: early expansion is followed by a relatively stable middle regime and substantial reorganization near the output. Class-relevant information is readable at shallow depth, while class-prototype separation strengthens mainly at later layers. Across datasets, stronger late dimensional contraction is associ-ated with greater increases in prototype separation. These findings distinguish the early availability of predictive information from the subsequent evolution of representation geometry. At the task level, datasets with later prediction stabilization tend to incur larger accuracy losses under truncation. The overall depthwise accuracy gain also cannot be attributed entirely to mismatch with the final decoder: across 72 OpenML-CC18 tasks, accuracy increases from 82.2% at L2 to 85.6% at L23 when each layer is evaluated using its own fitted readout. Additional computation therefore retains predictive value beyond what is accessible through shallow representations under these readouts. Finally, interventions show that the frozen model is sensitive to changes in its learned layer composi-tion. Replacing distinct blocks with repeated applications of a single pretrained block, or perturbing their execution order, alters representation dynamics and reduces predictive performance on aver-age. These results constrain simple post-hoc substitutions within the pretrained computation. Our findings support evaluating depthwise redundancy through input-dependent predictive gains along-side the accompanying representation dynamics, and motivate future work on adaptive-depth TFM inference. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Tabular Foundation Models. Tabular foundation models (TFMs) build on prior-data fitted net-works (PFNs) to perform in-context learning without task-specific parameter updates (Müller et al., 2022). TabPFN-v1 performs ICL over row-level representations, whereas TabPFN-v2 moves to cell-level representations with alternating row- and feature-wise attention; TabPFN-2.5 largely re-tains this design while increasing depth and scaling to larger tables (Hollmann et al., 2023; 2025; Grinsztajn et al., 2025). In parallel, TabICL introduces a compression-then-ICL architecture that first constructs fixed-dimensional row representations, with TabICLv2 further extending this design 2Preprint through repeated feature grouping and target-aware representations (Qu et al., 2025; 2026). LimiX expands structured-data foundation modeling through joint conditional prediction and retrieval-based ensemble inference (Zhang et al., 2025a), while TabPFN-3 combines feature grouping and row compression in a three-stage architecture (Grinsztajn et al., 2026). Despite this architectural progress, mechanistic understanding of TFM inference remains limited. Existing studies have ex-amined spectral adaptivity (Zheng et al., 2025), learned feature-space structure (Ye et al., 2025), intermediate-representation decodability (Hollmann et al., 2023), and layerwise predictive refine-ment and redundancy (Balef et al., 2026). Our work complements these studies by jointly examining representation geometry, sample-level prediction dynamics, and the functional role of depth. 

Layerwise Analysis of Transformer Representations. Layerwise analysis has been widely used to trace how information and computation evolve across Transformer depth. Probing and tuned readouts characterize when task-relevant information becomes accessible (Alain & Bengio, 2017; Hewitt & Manning, 2019; Tenney et al., 2019; Rogers et al., 2020; Belinkov, 2022; Belrose et al., 2023), while representation-similarity and geometric analyses characterize how internal representa-tions change across layers (Kornblith et al., 2019; Ansuini et al., 2019; Skean et al., 2025). Comple-mentary intervention-based studies test whether individual layers or their ordering are functionally consequential (Lad et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025). Most directly, Balef et al. (2026) identify early predictive accessibility, iterative refinement, and substantial depthwise redundancy in TFMs. We extend this layerwise perspective beyond predictive readability to characterize how geometry and individual prediction trajectories evolve, and whether later transformations retain functional value. 

Views of Computation Across Depth. Depth has been interpreted as both iterative refinement and structured representation transformation. Residual networks can implement successive refinements of existing representations (Jastrzebski et al., 2018), whereas geometric studies report nontrivial depthwise changes in dimensionality and information structure (Ansuini et al., 2019; Skean et al., 2025). Related work on shared transformations and adaptive-depth inference further asks whether computation can be repeated, truncated, or selectively allocated across inputs (Dehghani et al., 2019; Lan et al., 2020; Elbayad et al., 2020; Xin et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2020). These perspectives leave open whether TFM depth primarily reflects repetitive refinement or an ordered sequence of distinct, selectively useful transformations. 

## 3 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP AND ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK 

3.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Models. We use TabPFN-3 as the primary model for layerwise analysis (Grinsztajn et al., 2026). To assess cross-model generality, we additionally evaluate TabPFN-v2, TabPFN-2.5, TabICL, and TabICLv2 (Hollmann et al., 2025; Grinsztajn et al., 2025; Qu et al., 2025; 2026). Architectural details are provided in Appendix A.1, with cross-model results reported in Appendix C. 

Datasets and Evaluation Protocol. We evaluate binary and multiclass classification on 72 tasks from OpenML-CC18 (Bischl et al., 2019) and 200 tasks from TALENT (Liu et al., 2025). We restrict the study to classification to maintain a common output space for analyzing layerwise prediction dynamics and representation geometry. All models use their standard preprocessing and inference pipelines. The layerwise representation-extraction protocol is detailed in Appendix A.2. 3.2 LAYERWISE ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK 

Analysis framework. We analyze layerwise inference from three complementary perspectives: representation geometry, sample-dependent prediction dynamics, and the functional role of depth, building on prior layerwise probing, readout, and TFM analyses (Alain & Bengio, 2017; Belrose et al., 2023; Kornblith et al., 2019; Balef et al., 2026). For query i, we denote its hidden representa-tion after layer ℓ by h(ℓ) 

> i

and track its representation and prediction across depth. The representation-extraction protocol is detailed in Appendix A.2. 

Predictive readout. TabPFN-3 differs from earlier TabPFN variants in replacing the fixed-width classification head with an attention-based retrieval decoder (Grinsztajn et al., 2026). The decoder predicts directly from the relationship between query and support representations: support repre-sentations serve as keys, their one-hot labels as values, and query representations as queries. Be-3Preprint cause all 24 ICL layers preserve the same 512-dimensional hidden size and the decoder uses layer-independent projections, we can apply the same pretrained model-native decoder at every depth to obtain a consistent predictive readout across layers. To control for possible mismatch between this decoder and intermediate representations, we complement the native readout with layer-matched readouts. Full decoder definitions and validation results are provided in Appendix E.1. 

Representation and functional analyses. We characterize representation geometry using effective dimensionality (Ansuini et al., 2019), PCA displacement, linear CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019), and cosine similarity, capturing complementary aspects of dimensionality, movement, global structural similarity, and sample-wise directional alignment. We trace sample-level prediction trajectories and apply structural interventions to test when additional depth becomes functionally consequential (Lad et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025; Balef et al., 2026). Metric definitions are provided in Appendix B, with supplementary prediction-dynamics and functional analyses in Appendices G and H, respectively.

## 7 CONCLUSION 

Interpretation. Our study clarifies when later computation remains predictively useful and how its value varies across inputs. Class-relevant information becomes accessible at shallow depth, while representation geometry continues to evolve through an Expansion–Compression trajectory, with predictive accessibility preceding geometric resolution. At the sample level, predictions commit at substantially different depths, varying systematically with task difficulty and local ambiguity. This heterogeneity is reflected in the functional value of depth: locally ambiguous samples obtain larger aggregate gains from additional computation, later-committing tasks are more sensitive to trunca-tion, and depthwise predictive gains remain under layer-matched readouts. Structural interventions further show that later computation depends on an ordered sequence of distinct learned transforma-tions rather than generic repetition. These findings show that TFMs follow a shared computation path whose functional value varies substantially across inputs, motivating an input-dependent view of depthwise redundancy. 

Limitations and Future Work. Our analysis focuses primarily on TabPFN-3 and classification, although complementary experiments reveal related representation dynamics in several additional TFMs. The strength and temporal organization of Expansion–Compression may vary across archi-tectures and model scales. A practical early-exit mechanism will require reliable online signals that estimate whether additional computation is likely to alter or improve a prediction, rather than re-lying on the retrospective stabilization depth used in our analysis. Future work could investigate inference-time signals derived from prediction confidence, trajectory consistency, or representation geometry, and determine how reliably they identify inputs that benefit from continued computation. Extending these analyses to broader TFM architectures and regression settings is another impor-9Preprint tant direction, particularly because regression will require suitable continuous notions of predictive commitment rather than discrete class-identity stabilization.

## C CROSS -M ODEL GENERALITY OF EXPANSION –C OMPRESSION 

The main analyses focus on TabPFN-3 because its 24-layer ICL stack provides a particularly clear setting in which to study depth-dependent representation dynamics. We next examine whether the broad Expansion–Compression pattern also appears in other pretrained tabular foundation models. We consider four additional models: TabPFN-v2, TabPFN-2.5, TabICL, and TabICLv2. TabPFN-3 is omitted from the figures in this appendix because its representation trajectory is analyzed exten-sively in the main text. The purpose of the present analysis is instead to examine whether related geometric dynamics extend beyond the primary model. Across the four auxiliary architectures, the cross-dataset mean effective-dimensionality trajecto-ries exhibit the same broad qualitative ordering: effective dimensionality increases from its initial state, reaches an intermediate maximum, and subsequently decreases. The magnitude and tempo-ral organization of this trajectory nevertheless differ substantially across models. We therefore use Expansion–Compression to describe this recurring representation-space pattern across the models examined here, while allowing its strength and temporal organization to remain architecture depen-dent. Taken together, the cross-model results show that the Expansion–Compression pattern observed in the primary TabPFN-3 analysis is not unique to that architecture. Related non-monotonic ED trajectories are also present in TabPFN-v2, TabPFN-2.5, TabICL, and TabICLv2. At the same time, the models differ substantially in expansion strength, compression strength, the temporal concentration of representation movement, and the continuity of their pairwise represen-tation structure. The broad geometric pattern is therefore shared across the examined architectures, whereas its detailed organization is model dependent. The present comparison does not isolate a single architectural determinant of these differences. Model depth, hidden dimension, attention organization, decoder design, and pretraining procedure vary jointly across the model families. Identifying which of these factors determines the precise form of the trajectory therefore remains separate from the cross-model generality established here. Accordingly, the appropriate scope of the result is: the Expansion–Compression phenomenon ex-tends beyond TabPFN-3 within the tabular foundation models examined here, while its strength and temporal organization remain architecture dependent.

Within the models exam-ined here, these results support interpreting Expansion–Compression as a learned, depth-dependent organization of ICL representations rather than an automatic consequence of transformer depth.

Why the decoder is well-defined across depth. All 24 ICL layers preserve the same 512-dimensional hidden representation, and the decoder applies the same learned query and key pro-jections independently of layer index. The native decoder is therefore dimensionally well-defined at every ICL layer and provides a fixed, model-native readout across depth. This does not imply a fixed decision boundary: both query and support representations evolve with ℓ, changing their relative geometry and the resulting attention weights. 

Decoder-alignment concern. Dimensional compatibility does not imply that native-decoder per-formance measures all predictive information in an intermediate representation. Earlier layers may contain label-relevant information while remaining imperfectly aligned with the final decoder, mo-tivating tuned or layer-specific readouts (Belrose et al., 2023; Balef et al., 2026).
