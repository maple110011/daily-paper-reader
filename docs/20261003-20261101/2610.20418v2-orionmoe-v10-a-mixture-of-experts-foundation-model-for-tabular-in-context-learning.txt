## Abstract 

Tabular foundation models enable in-context learning by predicting unseen observations from a labeled support table without task-specific parameter updates. Despite recent advances, most existing approaches rely on dense architectures that apply the same transformations across features and datasets. We introduce ORION MOE-S MALL -V1.0, a compact tabular foundation model that combines distribution-aware representations with dataset-conditioned sparse mixture-of-experts computation. Built on a dual-axis transformer architecture, O RION MOE-S MALL selectively activates experts with routing decisions informed by both individual cell representations and the broader dataset context. This design enables adaptive allocation of computation across heterogeneous tabular problems while retaining a shared computational pathway across tasks. The model is pretrained entirely on synthetic classification tasks generated from a structured causal prior and performs downstream prediction without gradient-based adaptation. Experiments across multiple tabular benchmarks demonstrate competitive performance against substantially larger foundation models, with particularly strong results on small-context and high-dimensional datasets. Further analysis reveals favorable relative performance under class imbalance and highlights the importance of feature composition. 

Keywords: Tabular Foundation Model, Mixture-of-Experts, In-Context Learning, Tabular Classification

# 1 Introduction 

Tabular data remains one of the most prevalent forms of structured information in machine learning, underpinning applications in healthcare, finance, scientific discovery, and industrial systems [1, 2, 3, 4]. Unlike images or natural language, however, tabular datasets exhibit substantial heterogeneity in their statistical structure. Features may represent continuous measurements, categorical attributes, counts, or ordinal quantities, with widely varying distributions, scales, and predictive relationships. Dataset sizes, dimensionality, missingness, and label distributions also differ considerably across applications. This diversity has historically favored specialized learning algorithms, particularly gradient-boosted decision trees, over neural architectures that must be trained separately for each task [5, 6, 7, 8, 9]. Recent advances in tabular foundation models (TFMs) have begun to reshape this landscape. Models such as TabPFN [10, 11, 12, 13], TabICL [14, 15], OrionMSP [16], OrionBix [17], LimiX [18, 19], TabDPT [20], EXAONE Tabular [21] and TabFM [22] demonstrate that a single pretrained network can transfer predictive capabilities across diverse tabular datasets. A central paradigm underlying these models is in-context learning : given a labeled support table, the model predicts unseen observations through a forward pass, without task-specific parameter updates. By pretraining on large collections of synthetic prediction problems, TFMs amortize the cost of learning across tasks, replacing repeated optimization with contextual adaptation at inference time. Despite this progress, an important architectural question remains relatively unexplored: should every feature and dataset be processed by the same computational pathway? Existing in-context TFMs predominantly rely on dense transformer-style architectures, where feed-forward transformations are shared across feature representations and datasets. Although attention mechanisms allow these models to adapt their representations to the observed context, the underlying feed-forward computation remains dense and uniformly applied. Yet the statistical mechanisms governing tabular features can differ substantially, even within a single dataset. A continuous measurement, a high-cardinality categorical attribute, and a discrete variable may require different transformations to extract predictive information. Moreover, the relevance of a particular transformation may depend on the broader dataset context rather than on the individual feature alone. This raises the possibility that adapting the allocation of computation , in addition to the representations themselves, could provide a useful inductive bias for tabular foundation models. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures offer a natural framework for conditional computation. By selecting a subset of specialized networks for each input, sparse MoE models increase the diversity of available transformations without requiring every expert to be evaluated at every step [25, 26]. This principle has proved effective in scaling neural models, particularly in language modeling, where learned routing mechanisms allocate computation across different input representations. Its application to tabular in-context learning, however, raises a distinct challenge. Unlike language tokens, tabular feature representations have no universal semantics across datasets: the same numerical value may correspond to entirely different quantities, distributions, and predictive relationships. Consequently, expert selection should account not only for the local representation of a feature but also for the statistical context of the dataset in which it appears. In this report, we introduce O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0, a compact tabular foundation model that combines in-context learning with dataset-conditioned sparse computation. O RION MOE-S MALL retains the two-dimensional structure of tabular data through a dual-axis backbone that separates information exchange across observations from interactions among features and samples. Its central innovation is to replace dense feed-forward computation with a sparse mixture of experts. Each token is processed by a small subset of routed experts, allowing the model to select among multiple learned cell transformations while preserving a common computational pathway. ORION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 is pretrained entirely on synthetic prediction problems generated using an extended version of O’PRIOR [27], our synthetic task generator. The prior combines diverse structural causal mechanisms with heterogeneous feature distributions and controlled variations in dataset characteristics. This provides a broad family of learning problems in which different features and datasets may exhibit distinct statistical relationships, offering a natural setting for learning conditional feature transformations and dataset-aware routing. We evaluate O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 against recent tabular foundation models across multiple benchmark suites, including TabArena [24], OpenML-CC18 [28], TALENT [29], and BCCO [18]. # 2 Model Architecture and Inference 

ORION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 is a tabular foundation model that combines in-context learning with dataset-conditioned sparse computation. Given a labeled support set S = {(xi, y i)}Ni=1 and a set of unlabeled query observations

The architecture is motivated by two properties of tabular data. First, the meaning of an individual feature value depends on the empirical distribution and predictive relationships of the dataset in which it appears. Second, different features and datasets may require distinct computations to extract predictive information. O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 addresses these properties through a distribution-aware column encoder introduced in TabICLv2 [15], a dual-axis transformer backbone, and a sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) layer whose routing decisions are conditioned on a learned dataset representation. An in-context prediction head subsequently aggregates the learned representations to produce task-conditioned predictions. 

## 2.1 Model Architecture 

Distribution-aware column embedding. ORION MOE-S MALL represents a table as a grid of cell embeddings, retaining a separate representation for each observation–feature pair. Following TabICLv2 [15], a shared column encoder contextualizes each value using the support observations in its column. This provides a data-dependent representation of each feature without relying on fixed column identities. Specifically, scalar values are first projected into the model’s latent space and processed by a sequence of induced self-attention blocks. In each block, learned inducing vectors aggregate information from the support observations, and both support and query cells attend to the resulting column summaries. A cell’s representation therefore depends on its value and on the statistical context supplied by the support column: the same numerical value can receive different embeddings in different datasets. The column encoder is also target-aware. Support-target embeddings are added to the corresponding support representa-tions before induced attention, allowing the column summaries to capture information relevant to the prediction task. Query targets are never provided, and query observations do not contribute to the construction of the support summaries. We adapt this encoding scheme by processing columns individually, leaving cross-feature interactions to the subsequent dual-axis backbone. The contextual cell embeddings are combined with a learned missingness signal, preserving the distinction between absent entries and observed numerical values. In addition to the original feature tokens, O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 introduces learned feature registers. These registers participate in within-row computation and serve as dedicated representations for collecting information across the features of each observation. 

Dual-axis representation learning. A central design principle of O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 is to preserve the two-dimensional structure of tabular data. Each backbone block combines computation along the sample and feature axes, allowing representations to capture complementary dependencies. Along the row axis, attention operates across observations within each feature. This enables a cell representation to incorporate information from other observations belonging to the same dataset. Along the feature axis, attention operates across feature tokens within each observation, modeling dependencies among its attributes. This structure allows statistical information aggregated across observations and interactions among features to inform one another. Repeating these operations progressively integrates information across both axes while preserving individual feature representations. 3OrionMoE-Small-v1.0: A Mixture-of-Experts Foundation Model for Tabular In-Context Learning 

To support large in-context datasets, row-axis attention uses an induced set attention mechanism. A fixed number of learned inducing vectors first aggregate information from the support observations, and individual observations then attend to the resulting latent summaries. With a fixed number of inducing vectors, the row-axis attention cost scales linearly with the number of observations, rather than quadratically as in conventional full self-attention. The attention mechanism also enforces the in-context information boundary: support representations are computed exclusively from the labeled support set, while query representations attend only to support-derived information. Consequently, query observations cannot alter the support state used to predict other queries. Feature-axis attention, by contrast, operates directly over the feature tokens and learned registers. Together, the two attention mechanisms separate the aggregation of evidence across observations from the modeling of interactions among features. 

Sparse mixture-of-experts computation. The principal architectural contribution of O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 is the introduction of sparse conditional computation. Conventional dense tabular transformers apply the same feed forward transformation to every feature token, irrespective of the statistical properties of the feature or the dataset. ORION MOE-S MALL instead equips each block with a collection of expert networks and dynamically selects which transformations to apply. Dataset-conditioned expert routing. Beyond token-dependent expert selection, O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 introduces dataset-conditioned routing. The underlying motivation is that the appropriate transformation of a token may depend not only on its local representation, but also on the broader statistical structure of the table. To capture this information, the model maintains learned dataset registers r, which are progressively updated through attention-based aggregation over support representations. A register summarizes information about the current dataset and influences expert selection throughout the backbone. This mechanism distinguishes O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 from conventional token-only sparse routing. Two tokens with similar local representations may be assigned to different experts when they occur in different datasets. Conversely, the router may identify common computational requirements across different datasets and direct them to the same experts. Importantly, expert specialization is learned rather than prescribed: every routed expert remains eligible for every token, and routing is not constrained by feature type, dataset identity, or application domain. 

In-context prediction head. Following the dual-axis backbone, the feature registers are concatenated to obtain a fixed-width representation of each observation, which is combined with a projected representation of dataset registers. A stack of in-context attention layers then processes the resulting support and query representations. Support targets are incorporated into the context, allowing query predictions to depend on the relationships inferred from the labeled observations. For classification, a decoder maps the resulting query representations to class logits. Our model supports a native budget of ten classes and extends to larger label spaces through a hierarchical classification procedure. The resulting architecture combines distribution-aware cell representations, structured information exchange across the two axes of a table, and dataset-conditioned conditional computation within a single in-context prediction framework. At inference time, O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 adapts to a new dataset solely by conditioning on its labeled support table. The pretrained parameters remain fixed, and no task-specific optimization is required. The support/query separation also enables efficient reuse of support-derived representations across successive query batches. Since query observations cannot modify the support state, queries may be processed in chunks against a cached context without changing the resulting predictions, provided the support set and inference configuration are unchanged. 

## 2.2 Synthetic Prior Generation 

ORION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 is pretrained on synthetic in-context learning episodes generated using O’PRIOR [31], our previously released synthetic task generator. O’PRIOR provides a diverse collection of structural causal model families, including multilayer perceptrons, tree-based mechanisms, convolutions, Gaussian processes, time-lagged 4OrionMoE-Small-v1.0: A Mixture-of-Experts Foundation Model for Tabular In-Context Learning 

models, linear functions, and hybrid compositions. Together, these mechanisms capture a broad range of statistical relationships, from smooth nonlinear dependencies to discontinuous decision boundaries and temporal interactions. By varying the underlying structures, functional mechanisms, and sampling parameters, the prior generates prediction tasks spanning different dimensionalities, sample sizes, and levels of predictive complexity. Beyond the underlying causal mechanisms, O’PRIOR supports composable transformations that introduce hetero-geneous feature distributions, redundant attributes, outliers, different target distributions, and structured missingness. The generator also provides optional mechanisms for latent confounding, spurious correlations, and support–query distribution shifts. Transformations requiring fitted statistics are estimated exclusively from support observations, preserving the information boundary between support and query sets. For O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0, we additionally introduce controlled count-, ordinal-, and proportion-valued features, together with stage-dependent missingness patterns, to broaden the statistical conditions encountered during pretraining. The resulting prior exposes O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 to variation both within and across datasets. Heterogeneous generative mechanisms create opportunities for distinct feature-level transformations, while variations in dataset structure and statistical properties provide contextual information that can influence expert routing. This diversity is aligned with O RION MOE-S MALL ’s dataset-conditioned mixture-of-experts architecture, which learns to allocate computation based on both individual feature representations and the broader dataset context.

## 3.2 BeyondArena 

BeyondArena [33] evaluates tabular learning methods beyond conventional small-scale IID settings, incorporating grouped and temporal distribution shifts, larger datasets, and varying feature dimensionalities. Grouped evaluation measures generalization to previously unseen subpopulations, while temporal evaluation tests predictions on obser-vations collected after the training period. These settings provide a complementary perspective on the generalization capabilities of tabular foundation models, particularly when compared with conventional methods that can be optimized independently for each downstream task.

## 3.3 OpenML-CC18 

OpenML-CC18 [28] is a widely used benchmark suite comprising 72 classification tasks spanning diverse dataset sizes, feature dimensionalities, and class distributions. We follow the standard evaluation protocol provided by the benchmark library, retaining its predefined train–test splits, cross-validation folds, and default evaluation settings without modification. This ensures consistency with the established OpenML-CC18 evaluation procedure. To enable a fair comparison across the broadest set of participating foundation models, we first report results on the 48 tasks successfully completed by all compared methods, ensuring that Elo ratings and model rankings are computed over identical benchmark instances. The complete list of the 48 datasets is provided in Appendix B.1. Figure 5 reports the corresponding Elo ratings. O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 achieves an Elo of 1118 , behind Causilo (1170 ), and TabICLv2 ( 1121 ). In particular, O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 is within 3 Elo points of TabICLv2 and outperforms a broad set of recent foundation models, including TabLDM ( 1083 ), EXAONE-Tabular ( 1066 ), TabDPT-1.3 ( 1061 ), and TabPFN-3 ( 1015 ). These results place O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 among the strongest small models on this common-task evaluation, demonstrating that its compact, dataset-conditioned architecture can achieve competitive predictive performance against recent dense tabular foundation models.

To better understand the relationship between model capacity and predictive performance, we stratify the evaluated tabular foundation models into three parameter-count regimes: small ( < 50 M), medium ( 50 –150 M), and large (> 150 M). This analysis complements the aggregate benchmark comparisons by distinguishing performance against similarly sized architectures from performance against models with substantially larger parameter budgets. We use parameter count as a measure of model capacity, while recognizing that it does not directly capture active computation, pretraining cost, or inference efficiency.

O RION MOE-S MALL also outperforms the smaller EXAONE-Tabular ( 20 .8M) on OpenML-CC18, although EXAONE achieves higher ratings on the other benchmarks. Discussion. Overall, the capacity-stratified evaluation shows that O RION MOE-S MALL -V1.0 remains competitive within the small-model category and can match or outperform several medium-sized foundation models across multiple benchmarks. At the same time, Causilo’s stronger results at a comparable parameter count, together with the persistent advantage of the leading large models, indicate that substantial opportunities for improvement remain. The results support the practical viability of compact, dataset-conditioned sparse architectures, but do not establish that sparse routing itself is responsible for the observed performance differences.
