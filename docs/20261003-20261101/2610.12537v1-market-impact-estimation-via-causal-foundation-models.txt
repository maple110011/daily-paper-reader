## Abstract 

When a trader executes a large order, the relevant question is not 

what will the price be? but what would the price have been had I not traded? Market impact is thus a causal quantity, yet classical mod-els (Almgren–Chriss, propagators) regress price on flow, which is biased whenever informed flow moves prices through information as well as trading. We present a proof-of-concept Causal Market Simulator : a continuous-time causal prior-data fitted network (PFN) trained on a synthetic market prior in which a hidden informational flow confounds the flow–price relation by construction, with con-founding strength an exact per-episode knob. On this prior, the naive estimator’s bias follows a closed form we confirm empirically (up to 68% overestimation), while the PFN tracks the unbiased true-parameter oracle at high confounding. Its do-ablated twin , the same model with the intervention inputs removed, collapses onto the pre-window mean. Zero-shot, the model responds at the ground-truth scale to metaorders in an ABIDES limit-order book ( +0.38 pre-onset standard deviations against seed-replay counterfactuals), and its intervention response replicates on about 2,300 detected flow bursts in BTC/ETH/SOL perpetuals and 3,800 on Nasdaq equi-ties, evidence consistent with, though not proof of, causal transfer. Off-prior the response degrades gracefully, and split-conformal re-calibration repairs interval calibration. Transfer hinges on encoding the metaorder as added flow rather than a clamped price level, not on more training. A recurrent state-space variant closes the remain-ing shape gap: its impact accrues over execution duration where the transformer’s stays flat, raising the correlation with measured metaorder impact from +0.68 to between +0.92 and +0.96, at the cost of weaker on-prior deconfounding that traces to the training recipe rather than to recurrence.

## 1 Introduction 

Market impact, the price move caused by one’s own trading, is the central cost of execution and the canonical quantity of market microstructure. It is defined as the realized price path minus the counterfactual path that would have occurred without the trade [4]. Classical impact models are predictive approximations of this inter-ventional object. Almgren–Chriss fits a linear permanent-impact coefficient by regressing price on flow [1]. Propagator models fit a decay kernel to the same observational data [6, 16]. Both estimate 

𝐸 [𝑌 | 𝐴 ] where the object of interest is 𝐸 [𝑌 | do (𝐴 )] , the price under an imposed trade. The two differ whenever something moves both order flow and prices, and in real markets something always does [14]. Informed traders buy because they expect the price to rise, so their flow predicts price moves it did not mechanically cause. Esti-mated this way, impact coefficients absorb the information channel, and execution-cost models inherit the bias. Recent foundation models for microstructure learn rich observa-tional models of order flow at scale [22], but have no do-operator [17]: they answer “what typically happens next,” not “what happens if I trade.” Conversely, recent causal amortized-inference models, prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) trained to perform Bayesian causal inference in context, operate on tabular [3, 9, 11, 18, 19] or discrete-time [25] data. The continuous-time construction in [26] admits irregular event schedules as in markets, but like others has been evaluated primarily on synthetic or simulator-based tasks. That evidence gap is what our transfer tiers address. A generative line separately argued for causal market simulators [24]. What is miss-ing is the bridge: a continuous-time causal model, specialized to a market prior in which the causal estimand is provably distinct from the predictive one, and evidence that it transfers beyond its prior. This paper delivers that proof of concept. Our contributions: 

• A market prior with controllable confounding (§4). We split order flow into hidden informational core flow and en-dogenous reaction flow [14], and couple the core into the price through an informational channel 𝛾 . Per-episode con-founding strength is an exact, samplable quantity, and the naive estimator’s bias is available in closed form. It is linear in 𝛾 and zero when either channel closes [17]. 

• Implicit deconfounding, verified against an analytic law (§5). On episodes from this prior, the naive regression overestimates impact by up to 68% at strong coupling, match-ing the closed form within 2%. The causal PFN tracks the unbiased true-parameter oracle across the entire 𝛾 range, arXiv:2610.12537v1 [cs.LG] 8 Oct 2026 ICAIF ’26, November 14–17, 2026, Milan, Italy Thumm and Chen 

while its do-ablated twin, the identical model trained and evaluated with its five intervention inputs zeroed (§3), is statistically indistinguishable from the pre-window-mean baseline. The do-pathway, not the sequence model, carries the causal work. 

• Zero-shot transfer with matched semantics (§6). With-out any retraining, the same model responds at the ground-truth scale to metaorders [12] in an ABIDES [7] limit-order book (paired do-head effect, causal minus ablated twin, of 

+0.38 ˆ 𝜎 , 95% CI [0.34 , 0.42 ], 1,000 held-out seed-replay episodes) and reduces prediction bias around detected flow bursts on BTC/ETH/SOL perpetuals (paired do-head effect of +1.2– 1.5 ˆ 𝜎 over ≈760 events each; Table 1). Two mechanism find-ings enable the transfer: prior-scale standardization and matching the intervention’s semantics (additive flow, not a clamp) to the event’s (§6.1). 

• An architecture whose response accrues over execu-tion (§3). A recurrent state-space variant, gated on inter-observation time, composes impact in its recurrent state, lifting the 12-cell metaorder-benchmark correlation of §6.2 from +0.68 to +0.92– +0.96 across training seeds. 

## 2 Background and Related Work 

Market impact and execution. Almgren–Chriss decomposes im-pact into permanent and temporary components with linear co-efficients fitted to data [1]. Obizhaeva–Wang and the propagator literature model impact as a convolution of past order flow with a decaying kernel [6, 16]. Empirically, metaorder impact follows an approximate square-root law with slow decay [2, 6, 28], and the functional form is mechanism-dependent, zero then linear in equity auctions [21], which is why we treat the square-root law as a venue-specific benchmark rather than ground truth. Bonart [4] makes the causal structure explicit: impact is realized minus coun-terfactual return, square-root or linear depending on informational coupling. Muhle-Karbe et al. [14] supply a generative counterpart, decomposing order flow into exogenous informational core flow and endogenous reaction flow. We use Bonart’s identity as the estimand and the core/reaction split as the confounder structure. 

Causal amortized inference. Prior-data fitted networks meta-learn Bayesian inference by training on synthetic data drawn from a prior over generative processes [10, 15]. Do-PFN [19] extends this to in-terventional queries on tabular data, DoT-PFN [25] to discrete time, and CT-CPFN [26] to continuous time with irregular schedules. 

Microstructure foundation models and market simulators. TradeFM [22] trains a large generative Transformer on billions of trades, re-producing stylized facts [8] of order flow — 𝑝 (flow | history ), with no interventional semantics. ABIDES [7] provides interactive mar-kets with seed-replay counterfactuals (§6).

## 5 Synthetic Proof of Concept 

Protocol. We train the continuous-time causal PFN (4.2M param-eters, 5,000 steps, single A100 GPU, ∼2h) on the §4 prior, plus an 

ablated twin : the identical architecture, training prior, and train-ing seed with the five do-information inputs (target, kind, value, window ends) zeroed throughout training and prediction — a pure forecaster that knows where its context ends but not what happens there. All headline results (Fig. 2, Tables 1 and 2, except the marked recurrent-variant row of the latter) use one model: the skewed-𝛾 ,hard-intervention configuration of §4.2. The recipe paragraph below reports the ablation path that selected it. Evaluation draws suites of 500 episodes at each pinned 𝛾 ∈ { 0, 0.25 , . . . , 2} with 𝑠 = 0.7. All methods predict the same episodes’ exact counterfactual outcomes. Magnitudes here are in native prior units. The transfer tiers of §6 are standardized to pre-onset volatility units, written ˆ 𝜎 .

Baselines. The true-parameter oracle is a conditional-mean roll-out from the last pre-onset state given the episode’s true (𝜆, 𝜃 𝑈 , 𝜃 𝐴 , 𝜃 𝑌 ),with the hidden core at its stationary mean. It is unbiased for the in-terventional mean but not the minimum-RMSE Bayes predictor, so its RMSE is a conservative reference, not an exact floor. Further base-lines include Almgren–Chriss as the levels-regression permanent-impact estimator; an Obizhaeva–Wang-style (OW) fitted resilient propagator; a return-space regression of the horizon-matched re-turn on total signed flow (removing the levels regression’s filter-ing offset); the square-root law Δ𝑌 = 0.7 𝜎 pre √︁ |𝑄 |/ 𝑉 [6] (order size 𝑄 , volume 𝑉 , pre-onset volatility 𝜎 pre , written ˆ 𝜎 elsewhere) with zero fitted parameters; and last-value (AR1) and pre-window-mean predictors. Every classical baseline is fitted per episode on that episode’s pre-onset window only, and nothing is fitted across episodes or on post-onset data, in every tier. This family spans execution-cost models, the standard estimation recipe, and the em-pirical benchmark, so the bias is a property of observational fitting itself, not of one model class.

## 5.1 Robustness and controls 

What moved the residual bias — and what did not. Two nega-tive results shape the recipe: training 4 × longer changed nothing (0 .396 → 0.393 at 𝛾 =2), while shifting the training prior’s 𝛾 -mass toward high confounding cut bias by 57% (0 .396 → 0.169). or optimization. A matched training-side ablation completes the picture: retraining the identical configuration with soft interven-tions genuinely mixed 50/50 into the training prior roughly halves the unskewed configuration’s bias (0 .396 [0 .27 , 0.52] → 0.160–0 .191 at 𝛾 =2 across three training seeds). But it collapses the zero-shot BTC do-head response of §6 to a seed-dependent +0.02– +0.72 ˆ 𝜎 

(hard-trained: +1.42): the soft-trained response calibrates to the 

prior’s impact scale, which under-scales real markets, and semantics-matched training does not yield reliable transfer in this one-shot architecture. A magnitude sweep over all three soft seeds sharp-ens the reading: rescaling the encoded intervention 0 .25 ×–4 × on 173 burst episodes, every seed’s response rises monotonically from 0.5× and is significantly nonzero by 2 ×–4 ×, with attenuation rel-ative to the hard-trained response shrinking as magnitude grows (1 .3–1 .6× at 4 × on two seeds, ∼10 × on the weakest). Soft training recalibrates the gain toward the prior’s smaller 𝜆 -scale, and the gain is the seed-variable quantity, so the paradox is one of readout rather than a lost pathway. In the recurrent variant of §3, the same semantics-matched recipe helps on the metaorder benchmark (curve correlation +0.94 → + 0.96; §6.2). The one-shot transformer’s trans-fer evidence below thus rests on encoding-level generalization, not on semantics-matched training. 

Off-prior stress test. The evaluation above is on-prior by construc-tion, so we generate episodes from three generator families out-side the training prior’s model class — cyclic price →flow feedback d𝐴 += 𝜅𝑌 d𝑡 (𝜅 ∈ { 0.1, 0.2}), compound-Poisson jumps in the hid-den core, and saturating impact ( 𝜆𝐴 → 2𝜆 tanh (𝐴 /2)) — with exact shared-noise counterfactuals at 𝛾 =1. ## 6 Zero-Shot Transfer 

We now carry the trained model, unchanged, to an agent-based market with exact counterfactual ground truth (§6.1), to real market data (§6.2), and to interval calibration (§6.3), with Table 1 as the summary. 

## 6.1 ABIDES: counterfactual ground truth in an agent-based market 

ABIDES [7] provides what real data cannot: an exact counterfactual. We inject a metaorder agent (fixed-clock market orders, ∼25–40% participation over a 15-minute window) into a background market of noise, value, momentum, and adaptive market-making agents, and replay the identical seed without its orders. A silent twin of the metaorder agent keeps per-agent seeding and latency identical in the observational run. Pre-onset paths are bit-identical (asserted in automated tests) and post-onset divergence is the causal effect. Episodes are standardized to pre-onset- ˆ 𝜎 units. The intervention value is the metaorder’s standardized flow rate over the window. Each episode queries the price at one uniformly drawn post-onset bar (30 s bars, so horizons range from onset to session end) and 1,000 episodes across seeds. Because the two mechanism findings below (unit standardiza-tion, intervention semantics) were discovered by iterating on this environment, all exploratory analysis used seeds 0–999. The num-bers here are a single frozen evaluation on the disjoint held-out range 1000–1999 (model attribution: §3, “Two instantiations”). Ab-solute biases are noise-dominated at this scale, so Table 1 reports the

## 6.3 Counterfactual calibration 

Table 2 asks whether the quantile head’s counterfactual intervals, which point-estimate impact models cannot produce, are honest. In-distribution they are: coverage of the nominal 80% interval is 0 .77– 0.82 across the whole confounding range, at roughly one-third nar-rower intervals than the do-ablated twin — knowing the interven-tion sharpens counterfactual uncertainty, not just its point estimate. Under zero-shot transfer the picture inverts (causal coverage 0 .32 on ABIDES, 0 .47–0 .51 on crypto; ablated 0 .32–0 .39): point predictions transfer, uncertainty does not, because the prior’s outcome-noise scale is not the ABM’s or the market’s. The gap is aleatoric rather than epistemic: a predictive mixture over three training seeds covers 0.35 (transformer) and 0 .50 (recurrent) on ABIDES against single-seed values of 0 .33–0 .34 and 0 .46–0 .54, so ensembling is not the in-terval repair. Split-conformal recalibration [20] offers a concrete fix: widen both interval ends by an empirical quantile of held-out non-conformity scores 𝐸 𝑖 = max {𝑞 0.1 (𝑥 𝑖 )− 𝑦 𝑖 , 𝑦 𝑖 −𝑞 0.9 (𝑥 𝑖 )} , distribution-free and leaving point predictions untouched. On ABIDES the cal-ibration set can even be counterfactual (seed replay supplies the outcome under do (·) ): calibrating on seeds 0–499 and evaluating on 500–999 restores nominal-80% coverage from 0 .31 on that eval-uation half to 0 .82, at ∼4× wider intervals. On real markets only factual outcomes exist, so the same recipe carries a factual guaran-tee and counterfactual validity still rests on the prior — the honest boundary of what recalibration can fix. The recurrent variant’s raw ABIDES coverage is higher (0 .49 vs. the transformer’s 0 .32) and split-conformal restores 0 .81 at only 1.6× width (transformer: ∼4×) — but from a ∼3× wider base, so the transformer’s conformalized intervals remain the narrower ones in absolute terms (transformer 13 .1 vs. recurrent 16 .9 ˆ 𝜎 ), and, unlike in-distribution, the variant’s do-pathway worsens its raw ABIDES calibration against its own ablated twin (0 .49 vs. 0 .76 at equal width; Table 2). Its scale constant is likewise per-domain, not global: the crypto 𝑘 (0 .49–0 .53) does not transfer to ABIDES, where 

𝑘 is an order of magnitude smaller and varies with the training seed (0 .049–0 .096). Fitting 𝑘 on 𝑛 =500 calibration episodes and applying it to 𝑛 =1000 held-out seed-replay counterfactuals makes the calibrated bias seed-invariant at −0.12 ˆ 𝜎 with all three seeds’ 95% CIs spanning zero — though the calibrated response sits at the low end of the measured CI, so the transformer remains the better ABIDES model and the two instantiations keep their division of labor. Scale and interval calibration compose: recentering by 𝑘 

before conformalizing keeps coverage nominal (0 .783 at width 13 .4against 0 .812 at 16 .9 unscaled; 𝑛 =300 vs. 𝑛 =500) on counterfactual seed-replay ground truth. Cross-domain scale inference, predicting 

𝑘 from the observable tape instead of fitting it, remains open. Its labeling cost is small: on a Nasdaq closing-auction tier, five labeled auctions fit a 𝑘 whose held-out RMSE (0 .240) beats a last-value predictor (0 .280). 

## 7 Discussion and Limitations 

This is a proof of concept for the interventional axis of market foundation models: the prior is a stylized three-variable market with no order-book queueing, cancellations, or inventory, trades-as-drift-shifts idealize execution mechanics, the detected crypto events are only plausibly exogenous, and we do not compete with large observational models [22] on scale or realism. Interval calibration does not survive transfer (§6.3) even where point predictions do. Two further limitations: the recurrent variant’s schedule robustness outside regular bars requires schedule-mixture training (§3), and, as the placebo’s flip side, fed a mislabeled event the do-head confi-dently manufactures impact, so deployment requires trustworthy event labels. The consistent through-line, a positive paired do-head ICAIF ’26, November 14–17, 2026, Milan, Italy Thumm and Chen 

effect on synthetic, agent-based, and real data while the ablated twin collapses onto the mean, supports the narrower, load-bearing claim: a PFN can amortize causal inference under hidden confound-ing, where identification is supplied by the prior, not the data, and carry it zero-shot into markets it never saw, provided the prior’s confounding mass and intervention encoding matches the deployment event . Both fixes that mattered at fixed architecture were distribu-tion changes, not compute. The residual duration-shape gap was closed by injecting the intervention at every active step, which the recurrent variant completes (§6.2), and the residual level gap is a per-domain scale constant whose in-context inference remains open (§6.3). Within this proof of concept, a practitioner estimating execution cost should use the transformer pair, calibrated at the measured scale on the counterfactual ground-truth tier, with the recurrent variant supplying the duration profile. That no single instantiation yet achieves both is the sharpest open problem. The flip side of prior-based identification cuts both ways. In a companion experiment (identical recipe), enriching the prior with jump-diffusion in the hidden core suppressed the zero-shot do-head response roughly three- to eight-fold across the transfer tiers while fitting its own prior well: components that offer alternative ex-planations for intervention-sized moves dilute the prior-supplied identification, so predictive fit does not certify the causal read-out, and prior match , not richness, is the criterion. The converse exposure is misspecification: the deconfounding is only as trust-worthy as the model class the prior spans, and our prior assumes linear-drift OU dynamics where real microstructure is nonlinear, heavy-tailed, nonstationary, and shot through with feedback from price back into flow [5]. The stress test of §5.1 and the transfer tiers probe exactly this misspecification: feedback, jumps, and satura-tion lie outside the prior’s class, and neither ABIDES nor crypto is an OU system. The point-level causal response degrades grace-fully (bias shifts ≤ 0.16, §5.1) while calibrated uncertainty does not, where we would expect brittleness first. Widening the model class is the principled mitigation, and the framework [26] already admits neural-drift mechanisms. Excluded from the headline prior by design, since any realism layer must first pass a causal-attribution ablation: jump-diffusion drift (tested and rejected above), factor-structured multi-asset DAGs, and Hawkes-timed endogenous interventions. An ABM analogue of the 𝛾 -sweep and the rough/fractional scaling limit of [14] are future work.
