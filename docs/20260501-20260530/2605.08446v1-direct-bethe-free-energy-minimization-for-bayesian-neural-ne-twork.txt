# Abstract 

We propose training Bayesian neural networks by directly minimizing the Bethe free energy rather than maximizing a variational lower bound. On tree-structured factor graphs the Bethe free energy is exact; deterministic layers drop out of the objective and are trained by standard backpropagation, so the framework accommodates any mixture of probabilistic and deterministic subgraphs without modification. Restricting the weight posterior to a last-layer Gaussian yields analytically tractable losses: for a Gaussian likelihood the Bethe loss equals the exact marginal likelihood, and for a probit likelihood it reduces to a closed form via the probit–Gaussian convolution. Both objectives sit strictly between MAP and the ELBO ( LMAP ≤ L Bethe ≤ L ELBO ), removing the structural Jensen gap that no choice of variational family can close. The Z-consistent prior formulation makes the prior precision a differentiable parameter, enabling empirical Bayes — joint optimization of weights, covariance, and hyperparameters — in a single gradient pass, with no cross-validation or outer loop. All variants admit a closed-form predictive at MAP-equivalent inference cost, in contrast to ensemble and sampling-based methods. On 8 UCI regression and 12 UCI classification benchmarks evaluated under a single shared hyperparameter regime, Bethe is competitive with standard reference methods at single-pass cost. Independently, joint single-pass empirical Bayes matches grid-search cross-validation of the prior precision on essentially all dataset–variant combinations, eliminating the outer hyperparameter loop without measurable cost. Isolated optimization gaps on a few datasets reflect numerical rather than principled limitations of the framework. 

# 1 Introduction 

Bayesian neural networks are typically trained by maximising the evidence lower bound (ELBO)—a lower bound on the log-evidence obtained by Jensen’s inequality. The gap is structural: it closes only when the approximate posterior q equals the exact posterior, a condition never met in practice. A different objective arises from asking whether beliefs at neighbouring variables in the model agree—a local consistency condition. Beliefs satisfying this condition minimise the Bethe free energy [Yedidia et al., 2001, Minka, 2001]. On tree-structured factor graphs, local consistency is not merely a useful proxy: it is sufficient for exactness, the Bethe optimum is the true posterior, and the Jensen gap disappears. 

What direct Bethe minimisation unlocks. The Bethe free energy is usually treated as the objective that belief propagation implicitly minimises, accessible only through iterative message passing. We make it the explicit training loss and minimise it by gradient descent. For tree-structured graphs with last-layer Gaussian posteriors and Gaussian or probit likelihoods, the objective admits a closed form, and three practical consequences follow in a single gradient pass: (i) empirical Bayes —joint optimisation of weights, covariance, and prior hyperparameters with no outer cross-validation loop,

# 3 Direct Bethe Free Energy Minimisation 

Standard belief propagation finds stationary points of the Bethe free energy by iterative message pass-ing; on loopy graphs convergence is not guaranteed and the procedure is not end-to-end differentiable. We minimise FBethe directly by gradient descent. On tree-structured factor graphs this is exact—no approximation is introduced by the inference procedure itself, only by the choice of q and by the shared-cavity construction described below. 

Tree factor graph. Consider a factor graph whose variable nodes are weights {wl}, intermediate activations {hn,l }, and outputs {fn} (latent pre-likelihood scores), with inputs {xn} and observa-tions {yn} fixed (conditioned on). Factor nodes represent prior, transition, and observation factors. Any feedforward network without skip-connections induces such a graph and is therefore a tree; Equation (4) applies. 3Local consistency and the shared-cavity construction. Minimising FBethe is equivalent to finding local beliefs—one per factor, one per variable—that are mutually consistent at every edge: the marginal of each factor belief at a connected variable equals that variable’s belief. On a tree-structured graph, exact local consistency requires each factor to receive a per-edge cavity message . For the weight node w shared across N data plates, the exact cavity message to plate n is q(−n)(w) = q(w)/β n(w),where βn(w) ∝ R p(yn|fn) q(fn) d fn is plate n’s backward contribution. This per-plate cavity is exact but yields N distinct outgoing messages from w.We adopt the standard simplification of using the shared q(w) itself as the outgoing message to every plate—equivalent to assuming βn(w) ≈ 1 per plate, exact in the N → ∞ limit and tight when no single observation dominates the posterior. Because this approximation conflates per-plate cavities with the full posterior, all training in this paper uses full-batch gradients: each update consistently reflects every plate, whereas mini-batching would optimise the shared q(w) against subsets of plates and amplify per-plate cavity error. Empirical Bayes and special cases. The objective (5) spans a spectrum of inference regimes depending on how the prior factors − log Zwl are treated. Dropping them entirely recovers maximum-likelihood training with no prior on weights. Retaining them with fixed hyperparameters gives standard Bayesian inference under the Z-consistent prior, recovering MAP as beliefs concentrate. 4.3 Multiclass Classification 

Two strategies extend the binary Bethe formulation to K classes, both sharing the deterministic backbone ψn = NN θ (xn) and all covariance variants (V1–V3): (i) one-versus-all (OvA), which trains K independent binary probit heads and normalises at prediction time; and (ii) ordinal probit, which thresholds a single shared latent fn ∼ N (μn, v n) by K−1 learned boundaries to produce class probabilities via differences of Φ. For K = 2 the ordinal model reduces exactly to the binary loss Lclass (Theorem 2). Full derivations and loss expressions for both models are in Appendix A.3. 6Hyperparameter treatment. On regression concrete and yacht, the prior precision α runs to extreme values ( 10 3–10 5 for Bethe-FG; Table 14) under flat likelihood directions, degrading test NLL. We position the contribution by explicit contrast rather than a survey of Bayesian deep learning. 

Last-layer Bayesian neural networks. The closest baselines—VBLL [Harrison et al., 2024], Laplace Redux [Daxberger et al., 2021], and Rich-BLL [Calvo-Ordóñez et al., 2026]—all combine a deterministic backbone with a Bayesian last layer. VBLL trains an ELBO with a fixed regularisation weight; Laplace approximates the posterior post-hoc from the MAP solution; Rich-BLL uses an NTK approximation for richer last-layer expressiveness. Bethe differs in optimising a strictly tighter, closed-form objective with joint empirical Bayes on the prior precision in a single gradient pass, requiring no MC sampling or post-hoc Hessian estimation. 

Tighter variational bounds. IWAE [Burda et al., 2016] tightens the ELBO by importance-weighted sampling; Laplace Redux [Daxberger et al., 2021] and VBLL [Harrison et al., 2024] rely on per-step or post-hoc sampling. The Bethe objective is exact for Gaussian likelihoods (Theorem 1) and admits a closed form for probit (Theorem 2), removing the Jensen gap structurally rather than asymptotically. 

Probit and GP classification. The probit–Gaussian convolution that gives our closed-form classifi-cation loss is well known from GP classification [Williams and Rasmussen, 2006, MacKay, 1992], where it provides the predictive distribution at test time. We use it as a training loss with end-to-end optimisation of the feature map and posterior, rather than as a closed-form predictive over a fixed kernel. 

Empirical Bayes in deep learning. Type-II maximum likelihood for neural network priors [MacKay, 1992, Tipping, 2001] typically requires an outer optimisation loop or marginal-likelihood approximation. Z-consistency in our Bethe construction makes the prior precision a differentiable parameter of the loss, eliminating the outer loop. 

Belief propagation and EP. Bethe stationary points coincide with belief-propagation and EP fixed points [Minka, 2001, Yedidia et al., 2001], both reached by iterative message passing. We instead minimise the same objective by gradient descent, allowing end-to-end training jointly with the feature map. 

Sampling-based predictive uncertainty. Deep Ensembles [Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017] and MC Dropout [Gal and Ghahramani, 2016] require multiple forward passes at inference and do not target consistency. Bethe provides a closed-form predictive at MAP-equivalent cost; multi-pass refinements remain orthogonal and applicable on top. 

# 7 Conclusion 

We presented direct minimisation of the Bethe free energy as a training objective for Bayesian neural networks. The framework is general: on any tree-structured factor graph with tractable per-factor partition functions, the Bethe loss is exact and can be optimised end-to-end by gradient descent, accommodating any mixture of probabilistic and deterministic subgraphs. For the practical case of a Gaussian last-layer posterior with Gaussian or probit likelihoods, the loss admits a closed form; empirical Bayes optimisation of the prior precision happens in the same gradient pass that trains the model, predictives are closed-form (MAP-equivalent inference cost), and the resulting objective sits strictly between MAP and ELBO. On UCI regression and classification benchmarks, this single-pass instantiation matches or exceeds reference methods on a substantial fraction of NLL metrics; specific optimisation challenges and their diagnostic signatures are documented in Appendix B. 

Limitations. Our concrete instantiation is restricted to last-layer Gaussian posteriors over de-terministic backbones with Gaussian or probit likelihoods, and to UCI-scale benchmarks. The optimisation challenges documented in Appendix B— α-runaway, variance starvation in regression, validation-NLL early-stopping suboptimality, the OvA per-class α pathology, and the backbone-depth interaction—are open questions about the joint EB optimisation landscape rather than fundamental limitations of the Bethe framework itself. Probabilistic intermediate layers, non-Gaussian likelihoods, richer message families (particles, flows), and behaviour at larger scales remain to be addressed. 9References

# B Optimisation Failure Modes 

The Bethe objective is sound on tree-structured factor graphs (Section 3), but its joint optimisation of weights, covariance, and prior precision exposes specific failure modes that are not unique to Bethe but affect last-layer Bayesian neural networks more broadly. We characterise four such modes, each with a diagnostic signature in our experiments. For each we describe the mechanism, the empirical signature, and the open question; per-dataset diagnostic tables are in Appendices F and G. 

α-runaway. The empirical Bayes update for the prior precision α is unbounded above; gradient descent on Lreg or Lclass can drive α to extreme values ( 10 3–10 7) when the likelihood surface is flat in the direction the prior shrinks. Two regimes appear in our data. In the ill-conditioned regime, 

α-runaway correlates with degraded test NLL: on regression concrete and yacht, EB drives α to 

3,543 and 2.1 × 10 5 respectively (Bethe-FG), and Bethe trails the best baseline by 0.04–0.13 nats (Table 1). In the saturated regime, α-runaway is benign: on classification banknote, Ordinal-V2 reaches α = 1 .3 × 10 7 yet its test NLL ( 0.027 ) is the best on this dataset (DeepEns 0.029 ). The distinction is the conditioning of the likelihood relative to the prior, not the magnitude of α itself. This is consistent with the broader EB literature on the brittleness of Type-II maximum likelihood under near-flat likelihoods [Tipping, 2001, Molchanov et al., 2017]. A regularised EB update—bounding α

via a hyperprior or coupling its update step to the empirical posterior precision—is straightforward and we leave it to future work.

OvA per-class α on small multiclass. The OvA construction (Section 4.3) trains K binary heads each with its own prior precision αk. With K independent EB updates and small per-class samples, the αk estimates become individually noisy. The signature is in Table 8: on iris ( N = 150 , K = 3 ), OvA-V2 and OvA-V3 trail grid-CV by 0.153 and 0.164 nats respectively, while the corresponding Ordinal variants (which use a single shared α) trail by only 0.052 nats. A shared-α variant of OvA is a natural fix that we have not investigated; its derivation is straightforward, and we expect it to recover Ordinal-level EB performance on small multiclass benchmarks. 

Backbone interaction at depth. On 2-layer regression, reference methods (MAP, Laplace, Deep Ensemble) benefit substantially from added depth, while Bethe variants do not: the test-NLL improve-ment at 2L is 0.05 –0.50 nats for reference methods on most datasets but −0.10 to 0.20 nats for Bethe (full ∆-NLL table in Appendix F.7). The mechanism is plausibly that the variance-damped gradient through the deterministic backbone (Section 4) attenuates the signal that drives feature-map learning, an interaction between the Bethe last-layer loss and the upstream optimisation that does not arise under MAP-style point-estimate training. Whether this is a fundamental property of variance-aware loss landscapes or a tractable optimisation issue (e.g. via warmup or decoupled-update schedules) is open. We did not run 2-layer classification due to time constraints; the 1-layer pattern (where Bethe is competitive or best on 6 of 8 classification datasets, Table 2) suggests the depth interaction may be regression-specific, but this requires verification.

# D Experimental Setup 

D.1 Shared Training Protocol Architecture. One hidden layer, H = 50 units, tanh activations, no bias. Two-layer variants ( -2L )stack a second hidden layer of the same width with the same activation. 

Optimiser. Full-batch Adam, η = 0 .03 , up to 5,000 gradient steps. Early stopping monitors validation NLL with patience 50 and improvement threshold 10 −6; the checkpoint with lowest validation NLL is used for evaluation. 

Regularisation. L2 penalty λbb = 0 .01 on hidden-layer weights for all gradient-based methods. For MAP-type last layers (CE, Laplace, Deep Ensemble, VBLL), λll = 0 .1 on the last-layer weights. For Bethe methods, λll is replaced by an empirical Bayes prior whose precision α is jointly optimised (initialised at α0 = 1 ). Fixed-α variants ( -alpha: x) use a Bayesian prior with α pinned to x. These are explicit loss penalties, not decoupled weight decay. 

Numerical precision. All computations use 32-bit floating point. 

Seeds and data splits. Twenty independent runs, seeds s ∈ { 5, . . . , 24 }. Each seed controls both the data partition and the model initialisation, so the 20 repetitions estimate variability from both sources jointly. This differs from Rich-BLL, which fixes the data split and varies only the initialisation; see Appendix H.1. 

Pipeline deviations. We use full-batch gradients (instead of mini-batches) and tanh activations (required by the Bethe closed form). These choices slightly disadvantage methods that benefit from SGD noise (e.g. MC Dropout); we acknowledge this as a limitation of the comparison.

F.5 Oracle test NLL 

Bethe-FG shows oracle gaps of 0.05 –0.20 nats on regression (boston, energy, yacht), while reference methods stay within 0.05 nats. Together with the small classification oracle gaps (Appendix B), this localises the validation-NLL early-stopping suboptimality to the regression loss landscape. 

F.6 Summary 

The per-dataset diagnostic tables paint a consistent picture. RMSE (Table 12) is reported for completeness; Bethe variants are not optimised for point-prediction accuracy and may trade RMSE for improved calibrated likelihood. Calibration error (Table 13) shows that Bethe-Diag with joint σ2obs 

optimisation achieves the best calibration on most datasets; the fixed-sigma variant (Bethe-Diag-FS) is consistently worse-calibrated despite similar NLL, indicating that joint optimisation genuinely improves predictive calibration. The α and σ2obs tables together reveal the two failure regimes: 

α-runaway (concrete, yacht) manifests as extreme α with degraded NLL, while variance starvation (naval) manifests as collapsed σ2obs with large α but preserved NLL. The oracle table confirms that the Bethe objective itself finds good solutions—the gap is in early stopping, not in the loss landscape. 

F.7 Two-layer regression 

Table 17 reports absolute test NLL for two-layer models; Table 18 reports the per-seed difference (2L minus 1L, positive = 2L worse). Reference methods improve at 2L by up to 0.8 nats (Deep Ensemble on energy), while Bethe variants typically degrade by 0.05 –0.7 nats at depth. Since validation NLL improves at 2L for both Bethe and reference methods, the test-set degradation reflects validation overfitting of the joint EB dynamics rather than objective inadequacy (Appendix B).
