(Dated: October 6, 2026) We present a proof-of-concept study of neural ratio estimation (NRE) — a simulation-based in-ference algorithm — applied to WIMP direct detection using the XENONnT experiment as an example. A binary classifier is trained on simulated parameter–observation pairs to approximate the likelihood ratio for the WIMP mass mχ and spin-independent proton coupling cp, using realis-tic scintillation and ionization signals produced by the fuse detector simulation. We demonstrate that astrophysical uncertainties from halo model variations are marginalized over implicitly and at no additional inference cost by including multiple halo models in the training data. Using ex-treme ±5σ Standard Halo Model variations, we show that training on a combined dataset recovers well-calibrated posteriors even under severe misspecification, while more realistic halo models pro-duce nearly indistinguishable results. The signal-plus-background classifier yields an exclusion limit that is both qualitatively and (after accounting for differences between frequentist and Bayesian ap-proaches) quantitatively consistent with the XENONnT expected sensitivity. These results establish NRE as a scalable complement to the profile likelihood ratio approach, with particular advantages as experiments probe deeper into the neutrino fog. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

Understanding the particle nature of dark mat-ter (DM) is among the central challenges in modern physics. Within the ΛCDM cosmological framework, DM accounts for approximately 27% of the total energy bud-get of the present universe [1], yet no particle within the Standard Model (SM) can explain its observed prop-erties. Weakly Interacting Massive Particles (WIMPs) remain among the most theoretically compelling candi-dates [2]: a thermally produced particle with weak-scale mass and couplings naturally reproduces the observed relic abundance via freeze-out. Direct detection experiments search for the rare scat-tering of galactic WIMPs off nuclei, converting the result-ing nuclear recoil energy into measurable signals. Lead-ing experiments based on dual-phase liquid xenon (LXe) time projection chambers — XENONnT [3], LZ [4], and PandaX-4T [5] — exploit the simultaneous detection of scintillation (S1) and ionization (S2) signals for three-dimensional position reconstruction and powerful dis-crimination between nuclear recoil (NR) signal and elec-tron recoil (ER) background. Despite decades of ex-perimental effort, no convincing WIMP signal has been observed. The most stringent current bounds on the spin-independent (SI) WIMP–nucleon cross section reach 

σp = 2 .2 × 10 −48 cm 2 at 40 GeV /c 2 [6].

The conventional statistical framework for WIMP searches is the extended profile likelihood ratio (PLR) test, in which signal and background probability density functions are built from detector simulations and calibra-tion data, and nuisance parameters encoding systematic uncertainties are profiled over. To reduce complexity, astrophysical uncertainties from the local DM velocity distribution are commonly not included and the rele-vant parameters are simply fixed to the Standard Halo Model (SHM) central values without profiling. As the sensitivities of direct detection experiments improve this approximation will need to be revisited [7]. However, each additional nuisance parameter increases the dimen-sionality of the profiling problem and the computational cost of toy-Monte Carlo (toyMC) based inference, which already requires 10 4–10 5 pseudo-experiments per tested cross section to characterize the sampling distribution of the test statistic. Simulation-based inference (SBI) [8, 9], originally named likelihood-free inference, addresses this challenge by training neural networks on simulated parameter– observation pairs ( ϑ, x) to learn a surrogate for the like-lihood ratio or posterior directly, without requiring an explicit expression for the likelihood. Once trained, in-ference is amortized : the network evaluates new ob-servations at negligible cost. A particularly attractive feature is that nuisance parameters are absorbed im-plicitly into the training distribution by sampling them from their allowed ranges at simulation time and dis-carding them before training. The resulting marginal-ization is exact in the infinite-data limit and requires no modification of the inference step [8]. SBI has in-arXiv:2610.06159v1 [hep-ph] 5 Oct 2026 2creasingly been used in searches for dark matter [10– 15], but also in other branches of physics, from gravi-tational wave astronomy [16] to cosmological parameter inference [17] and searches for physics beyond the Stan-dard Model [18, 19]. In this paper, we present a proof-of-concept applica-tion of neural ratio estimation (NRE), a specific SBI al-gorithm that approximates the likelihood ratio via a bi-nary classifier, using the XENONnT experiment as an example. We use a realistic signal model with WIMP spectra generated by WimPyDD [20] and detector re-sponse modeled by the publicly available XENONnT de-tector simulation fuse [21]. NRE is chosen over alter-native SBI algorithms, such as neural posterior estima-tion (NPE), which directly learns a generative model of the posterior [9], because the learned likelihood ratio is prior-independent and can be more easily compared with conventional frequentist approaches in the context of ex-clusion limit setting. Beyond demonstrating that NRE yields exclusion lim-its compatible with the established PLR approach, our central contribution is a systematic study of astrophys-ical uncertainty marginalization across a range of halo models — from extreme ±5σ SHM variations to obser-vationally motivated models including the SHM ++ [22] and a Large Magellanic Cloud (LMC) contribution [23]. A key practical advantage is scalability: incorporating additional nuisance parameters requires only diversify-ing the training data, leaving the cost of inference un-changed. The paper is organized as follows. Sec. II introduces the simulation pipeline, the data representation, and the NRE algorithm. Sec. III presents signal-only results to-gether with a discussion of posterior reliability. Sec. IV extends the analysis to the signal-plus-background case and provides a prior-independent frequentist cross-check. Sec. V develops the astrophysical uncertainty assessment. Sec. VI summarizes the results and discusses future ex-tensions. Appendix A details the network architectures and hyperparameters, Appendix B presents an analyti-cal validation on idealized 1D spectra, and Appendix C defines the posterior diagnostic tools.

A. Posterior reliability 

Trustworthy inference requires more than a low train-ing loss. The most rigorous check performed in this study is the comparison to the analytical Poisson posterior in the idealized 1D case described in Appendix B, which uses true recoil spectra provided by WimPyDD without detector simulation to determine the event rate per bin deterministically. That validation shows excellent agree-ment, confirming that the NRE learns an accurate surro-gate. In the full detector simulation setting, no such an-alytical reference exists; we instead rely on coverage tests and distance metrics, described in detail in Appendix C. Coverage tests assess calibration by checking whether true parameter values fall inside the claimed credible re-gions with the correct frequency. A well-calibrated pos-terior should contain ϑtrue within its α-credible highest posterior density (HPD) region in exactly a fraction α

of cases; deviations indicate posteriors that are system-atically too broad or too narrow. Fig. 5 shows cover-age curves for both the signal-only and the signal-plus-background classifier (discussed below), each evaluated on 5,000 test spectra. The signal-only case follows the diagonal almost perfectly. The estimator ˆ cp is pushed to zero whenever the data fluctuate below the background-only expectation. Under the background-only hypothesis, the test statistic −2 ln λ therefore follows the boundary mix-ture 12 δ(0) + 12 χ21 [30], which leads to a one-sided 90% threshold of −2 ln λ ≤ 1.64 or

λ is equal to ∆. We note that this limit can only be obtained above a certain critical mass mmin . Below this mass, the experiment loses sensitivity and the averaged posterior becomes flat in cp, such that λ never drops be-low ∆. Most impor-tantly, we have only included a flat ER background, while the full XENONnT analysis accounts for additional sources (accidental coincidences, surface events, radio-genic neutrons) which differ from a DM signal not only in their (cS1, cS2) morphology, but also in their spatial distribution, which is not considered in our analysis. 

V. ASTROPHYSICAL UNCERTAINTIES 

The event rate for DM scattering in Eq. (1) depends on the astrophysical inputs ρχ and f (v). While ρχ en-ters only as an overall normalization [7], the velocity dis-tribution f (v) enters in a non-trivial way through the halo integral η(vmin ) in Eq. (3). Uncertainties in f (v)are most consequential at low DM masses, where the re-duced momentum transfer pushes vmin into the exponen-tially suppressed high-speed tail of η(vmin ), and different halo models may predict substantially different rates. In conventional PLR analyses these uncertainties are usu-ally not included, because doing so would require addi-tional profiling dimensions and a corresponding increase in toyMC cost. In NRE, by contrast, halo model nuisance parameters η are handled implicitly and at no additional inference cost. For each simulation, η is drawn from its prior p(η) independently of the particle physics param-eters ϑ, but the chosen value of η is discarded before training. The training data are therefore samples from the marginal distribution 

p(x, ϑ) = p(ϑ)

Z

p(x|ϑ, η) p(η) dη , (9) and the learned surrogate automatically approximates the marginalized likelihood p(x|ϑ), with the halo uncer-tainty absorbed into the diversity of the training data.

We consider the halo models summarized in Tab. II. The SHM serves as the baseline reference through-out. The SHM ++ [22] adds a radially anisotropic Gaia Sausage component reflecting the kinematic signature of an ancient dwarf galaxy merger. The LMC model [23] ac-counts for the perturbation of the local DM distribution by the first pericentric passage of the Large Magellanic Cloud. To probe the robustness of the inference pipeline under severe misspecification, we additionally construct two extreme benchmarks as ±5σ excursions in the SHM nuisance parameters ( v0, vesc ): SHMhigh, with a velocity distribution pushed to higher velocities, and SHMlow, with lower velocities. We emphasize that these models are not physically motivated but serve to illustrate the implicit marginalization procedure. B. Realistic halo models 

We now repeat the analysis for the three realistic halo models SHM, SHM ++ , and LMC, with SHM as the ref-erence, and additionally train a combined classifier, for which all three halo models are mixed with equal proba-bility. > VI. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS

In this work we have demonstrated that simulation-based inference is a powerful tool for the analysis of di-rect detection experiments. Specifically, we have applied neural ratio estimation (NRE) using the XENONnT ex-periment as an example and provided a proof of concept that the resulting exclusion limits are qualitatively con-sistent with those obtained using the conventional pro-file likelihood ratio (PLR) approach without the need for an analytic likelihood function or expensive Monte Carlo simulations. The signal-plus-background NRE classifier trained on simulated data yields a 90% CL sensitivity projection that approximates the official XENONnT expected sen-sitivity. Most of the deviation can be attributed to inher-ent differences between the Bayesian and frequentist ap-proaches and largely disappears in the frequentist cross-check via the profiled likelihood ratio of Eq. (7), which removes prior volume effects. The residual difference re-flects our simplified background model and the fact that no position information is used in our analysis. We con-clude that the NRE procedure recovers the same physi-cal information as the PLR approach at potentially lower simulation cost. Indeed, the key advantage of NRE over the PLR ap-proach is its scalability. In NRE, additional nuisance parameters are incorporated simply by diversifying the training data, leaving the cost of inference unchanged. This is particularly valuable as experiments approach the neutrino fog [31], i.e. the irreducible background from co-herent elastic neutrino–nucleus scattering (CE νNS). In this parameter region, the correct simultaneous propaga-tion of CE νNS flux uncertainties, halo model variations, and instrumental systematics becomes essential. In the present work we have focused on astrophysi-cal uncertainties from the dark matter velocity distri-bution. In NRE, uncertainties in this distribution can be marginalized over implicitly by combining training datasets from multiple halo models or from variations in the halo model parameters. Coverage tests on extreme benchmarks confirm that this procedure corrects under-coverage from halo misspecification: While a classifier based on an incorrect halo model yields posteriors that tend to falsely exclude the true model parameters, a clas-sifier trained on a combination of different models yields well-calibrated results. We have then applied this approach to realistic halo models based on both observations and simulations. These models produce nearly indistinguishable results in the current sensitivity regime, with differences at the 

O(20%) level in terms of the excluded cross section for low dark matter masses. The NRE approach removes the need to select one of these halo models, making it possible to instead include all of them in the analysis simultaneously. Our current NRE implementation has several limita-tions that point toward a clear path forward. The coarse 10 ×10 histogram treats the 2D (cS1, cS2) observable as a flat vector, discarding spatial relationships between bins. A convolutional neural network would exploit these cor-relations at much higher resolution without the quadratic input-dimension scaling that constrains the multilayer perceptron. Alternatively, a transformer architecture [32] acting on the unordered set of (cS1, cS2) event pairs could bypass binning entirely. Before our approach can be applied to real XENONnT data, it will be necessary to extend the background model to include the full set of background sources with their rates as nuisance parameters. The background model uncertainties can then be marginalized over through the same implicit mechanism already employed for halo un-certainties. However, several of these backgrounds are not uniformly distributed in the detector volume, such that position information becomes essential for signal dis-crimination. At the same time, it will also be interesting to con-sider a wider range of possible particle physics properties of dark matter. Our framework can be easily extended to other effective operators for dark matter–nucleus scat-tering beyond standard spin-independent interactions. In fact, it would also be straightforward to include several operators simultaneously, for example to interpret poten-tial excesses in the data. Such excesses may well appear in the planned XLZD observatory [33], which will probe deep into the atmospheric neutrino fog. This is precisely the regime where simulation-based inference with its im-plicit marginalization over growing systematic uncertain-ties offers its greatest practical advantage. 10

> Appendix A: Neural Network Architectures and Training

Tab. III collects the hyperparameter configurations for the three primary classifiers: the signal-only and signal-plus-background classifiers trained on (cS1, cS2) data and discussed in the main text, and the idealized classifier trained on WimPyDD spectra and discussed in Appendix B. Hyperparameters are optimized with Optuna [34] using the Tree-structured Parzen Estima-tor (TPE) sampler and a median pruner, running for up to ∼ 200 trials per classifier. The TPE sampler builds a probabilistic model of the objective function to pro-pose promising configurations, while the median pruner terminates unpromising trials early based on intermedi-ate validation losses. All models use SiLU activations, batch normalization after each hidden layer, AdamW op-timization [35] with an exponential learning rate decay, and early stopping with a patience of 20 epochs. The training split is 80% training, 15% validation, 5% test. The number of histogram bins nbins for the (cS1, cS2) classifiers was not included in the formal hyperparame-ter optimization. Since the MLP treats the histogram as a flat vector, spatial correlations between neighbor-ing bins are not exploited, and finer binning primarily increases the input dimension quadratically without a commensurate gain in discriminating power. We there-fore fix nbins = 10 throughout; CNN-based architectures, which can exploit local spatial structure, would be bet-ter suited to benefit from higher resolutions. → 0.411 (full detector, signal only) → 0.585 (signal plus background) — quantifies the incremental inference cost of each modeling step.
